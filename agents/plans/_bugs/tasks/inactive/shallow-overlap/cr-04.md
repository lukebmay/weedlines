# cr-04

Two defects remain in the change. The named checks passed.
One border is reported as one separate-figure overlap when
the shared area is four regions. Two rectangular regions
with a gap between them are reported as one.

## Defects

1. A radius-10 circle centered at the origin is one figure.
   The other figure is a ring. The outer border has
   vertices at (15, 0), (0, 15), (-15, 0), and (0, -15).
   The inner border has vertices at (10, 0), (0, 10),
   (-10, 0), and (0, -10), and that border is the hole.
   The outlines meet at those four inner vertices and do
   not cross. The crossing list is empty.

   The origin lies in the circle and not in the ring. The
   point (12, 0) lies in the ring and not in the circle.
   This is not nesting.

   The interiors share four regions. The points (6, 6),
   (6, -6), (-6, 6), and (-6, -6) each lie in both. The
   straight segment from (6, 6) to (6, -6) leaves the
   ring: (5.833, 0) lies in the circle and not in the
   ring. Near (10, 0), (9.99, 0.02) and (9.99, -0.02)
   lie in both interiors, and (9.99, 0) lies in the
   circle only. The same split is at the other three
   inner vertices. A fill grid around each vertex, with
   a step of 0.01, is two components. The regions meet
   only at the vertices. A point touch is not shared
   area. There is no dangling end and no self-overlap.

   The figure check reports one separate-figure overlap.
   The target is about (-5.833, -5.833). The other three
   regions have no target. The intersection polygon has
   four pieces, near (5.833, 5.833), (-5.833, 5.833),
   (-5.833, -5.833), and (5.833, -5.833). Each of those
   points lies in both interiors.

   `_contained_side` in `src/weedlib/figures.py` keeps
   every sample of one border as one region when the
   piece midpoints lie in the other fill. The inner
   border's midpoints lie in the circle, and that border
   passes through all four regions, so the four pieces
   become one separate-figure overlap.

   Remedy: split that border where the straight segment
   between consecutive samples leaves either interior.
   Each connected run is one region. A piece of the
   intersection polygon joins only the run it meets
   through both interiors. These four regions are four
   separate-figure overlaps. The 64-sided ring stays
   one: at radius 9.995 both interiors contain the whole
   circle, and consecutive samples along it do not leave
   either interior. The radius-10 circle and the
   radius-3 circle with centers 7 apart stay nesting.

2. One figure is the rectangle from (0, 0) to (30, 4).
   The other figure has two closed subpaths: the
   rectangle from (-2, 1) to (8, 3), and the rectangle
   from (8.5, 1) to (32.5, 3).

   The point (-1, 2) lies only in the two-rectangle
   figure. The point (31, 2) lies only in that figure.
   The point (15, 0.2) lies only in the wide rectangle.
   This is not nesting.

   The interiors share two regions. (4, 2) lies in both.
   (19, 2) lies in both. (8.2, 2) lies in the wide
   rectangle and not in the other figure. The regions
   do not meet. There is no dangling end and no
   self-overlap.

   The figure check reports one separate-figure overlap.
   The target is (4, 2). The region that contains (19, 2)
   has no target.

   `_segment_in_both` in `src/weedlib/figures.py` probes
   16 points on the straight segment. From (4, 2) to
   about (19.25, 2) those probes miss the gap, so
   `_one_point_per_region` joins the two regions and
   keeps the point with the smaller coordinates.

   Remedy: a straight segment joins two regions only
   when the points along it stay in both interiors.
   Sixteen probes on a long segment are not enough for
   the gap at (8.2, 2). These two regions stay two
   separate-figure overlaps.
